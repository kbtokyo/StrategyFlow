Explore the Framework

TAM

Total Addressable Market

  • Full potential market size
  • Assumes 100% market share
  • Broadest view of the opportunity
  • Includes all geographies, segments & use-cases
  • No constraints on reach or execution
SAM

Serviceable Addressable Market

  • The portion of TAM you can actually reach
  • Filtered by geography & tech capability
  • Accounts for regulatory constraints
  • Removes customers you cannot serve today
  • Defines your realistic competitive arena
SOM

Serviceable Obtainable Market

  • The share you can realistically win
  • Based on current traction & resources
  • Factors in competitor landscape
  • Grounded in conversion rate estimates
  • Drives near-term revenue forecasts
TAM

Why Calculate TAM?

  • Show big opportunity to investors
  • Understand the long-term ceiling
  • Evaluate if a market is worth entering
  • Set long-term strategic ambition
  • Justify R&D and infrastructure spend
SAM

Why Calculate SAM?

  • Set practical growth goals
  • Plan go-to-market or expansion strategy
  • Show limits honestly to investors
  • Prioritise which segments to target first
  • Align stakeholders on medium-term potential
SOM

Why Calculate SOM?

  • Plan hiring and resourcing decisions
  • Set realistic revenue targets
  • Track achievable growth milestones
  • Build credible sales forecasts
  • Align team budget planning
1

Estimating TAM

  • Identify all potential customers globally
  • Multiply by average annual spend or price
  • Use top-down: find industry research reports
  • Or bottom-up: count all possible buyers × ARPU
  • Validate with analyst data (Gartner, IDC, etc.)
2

Estimating SAM

  • Start from TAM
  • Remove unreachable users (language, regulation)
  • Filter by geographies you can operate in
  • Factor in pricing — remove those who can't afford it
  • Consider what you can truly support today
3

Estimating SOM

  • Look at current growth rate & trajectory
  • Estimate conversion from your target audience
  • Factor in competitive landscape & win rate
  • Use historical sales data where available
  • Apply 3–5 year capture horizon realistically
📊

Use TAM when…

  • Pitching in investor decks (Series A+)
  • Doing product visioning & roadmapping
  • Forming a long-term market strategy
  • Evaluating new verticals to enter
  • Communicating mission scope to the board
🎯

Use SAM when…

  • Aligning stakeholders on short/medium term potential
  • Planning go-to-market launch strategy
  • Building 18–36 month growth plans
  • Identifying which customer segments to focus on
  • Comparing competitive positioning
🏆

Use SOM when…

  • Building quarterly OKRs or sales plans
  • Creating sales/revenue forecasts
  • Aligning team and budget planning
  • Reporting to investors on near-term milestones
  • Setting hiring headcount targets
TAM Risk 1
📈

Using Inflated or Outdated Stats

Relying on stale market reports or cherry-picking the largest figure makes TAM look bigger than it is, eroding investor trust.

TAM Risk 2
⚠️

Ignoring Future Saturation

Markets mature and get disrupted. A static TAM ignores shrinkage from regulation, technology shifts, or saturation over time.

SAM Risk 1
🌍

Underestimating Localisation Barriers

Language, culture, compliance, and payment infrastructure often reduce SAM dramatically — especially for cross-border products.

SAM Risk 2
🔮

Overpromising Potential

Assuming you can reach all customers in a geography ignores distribution gaps, brand awareness, and operational constraints.

SOM Risk 1
🏎️

Overestimating Short-Term Ability

New ventures often lack the sales capacity, brand trust, and distribution to capture as much SOM as projected in Year 1–2.

SOM Risk 2
🔄

Ignoring Churn & Switching Friction

Winning customers is only half the battle. High churn or low retention can mean SOM shrinks even as you acquire new users.
